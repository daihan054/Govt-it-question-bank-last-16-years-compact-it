7. **(b) What is register? What are the common register found in a basic computer?** *[BPSC (Ministry of Power, Energy & Mineral Resources) Assistant Director (ICT) (CS/CSE) 29.05.2025 compact it 1356 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer:

   What a register is:
   - A register is a very small, very fast storage location inside the CPU itself, used to hold data, an address or an instruction while it is being processed. It is the fastest storage in the memory hierarchy, faster than cache and main memory, but there are only a few of them and each holds only a word.

   Common registers in a basic computer:
   - Accumulator, AC: holds one operand and the result of arithmetic and logic operations performed by the ALU.
   - Program Counter, PC: holds the address of the next instruction to be fetched.
   - Instruction Register, IR: holds the instruction currently being decoded and executed.
   - Memory Address Register, MAR: holds the memory address from which data is to be read or to which it is to be written.
   - Memory Buffer Register, MBR, also called the Memory Data Register: holds the data just read from or about to be written to memory.
   - Data Register, DR: holds the operand read from memory.
   - Address Register, AR: holds the address of the operand in memory.
   - Temporary Register, TR: holds intermediate values during a computation.
   - Input Register and Output Register, INPR and OUTR: hold a character coming from an input device or going to an output device.
   - Status or Flag Register, PSW: holds condition flags such as zero, carry, sign and overflow.
   - Stack Pointer, SP: holds the address of the top of the stack.
   - General purpose registers, R0 to Rn, available to the programmer for any purpose.
8. **1TB = কত বাইট?** *[BPSC Ministry of Women and Children Affairs Computer Trainer 2021 compact it 944 (ET: N/A)], [BARI Assistant Maintenance Engineer 15.11.2025 compact it 1451 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer: 1 TB equals 1,000,000,000,000 bytes in the decimal system and 1,099,511,627,776 bytes in the binary system.

   - Decimal, the SI convention used by disk manufacturers: 1 TB = 10¹² bytes = 1,000 GB.
   - Binary, the convention used by operating systems: 1 TB = 2⁴⁰ bytes = 1024 GB = 1,099,511,627,776 bytes. Strictly this quantity should be called a tebibyte, TiB.
   - This is why a disk sold as 1 TB shows about 931 GB in Windows: the manufacturer counted in powers of ten and the operating system counts in powers of two.
   - The chain: 1 KB = 1024 bytes, 1 MB = 1024 KB, 1 GB = 1024 MB, 1 TB = 1024 GB.

   Answer:

   What a computer generation means:
   - A computer generation is a stage in the development of computers, marked by the electronic component used to build the processor and the resulting change in size, speed, cost and reliability. Each generation represents a fundamental change in technology rather than a mere improvement.

   Characteristics of the generations:

   | Generation | Period | Main component | Characteristics |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | First | 1940 to 1956 | Vacuum tube | Very large and heavy, filling a room; enormous power consumption and heat; magnetic drum memory; programmed in machine language; very expensive and unreliable. Examples: ENIAC, UNIVAC, EDVAC |
   | Second | 1956 to 1963 | Transistor | Much smaller, faster, cheaper and more reliable than tubes; less heat; magnetic core memory; assembly language and early high level languages such as FORTRAN and COBOL. Examples: IBM 1401, IBM 7094 |
   | Third | 1964 to 1971 | Integrated Circuit, IC | Many transistors on one chip, so far smaller and faster; keyboard and monitor replaced punched cards; operating systems allowed multiprogramming and time sharing; high level languages became standard. Examples: IBM 360, PDP-8 |
   | Fourth | 1971 to present | Microprocessor, that is LSI and VLSI | Thousands and then millions of transistors on one chip; the personal computer became possible; GUI, mouse, networks and the Internet; semiconductor memory; very cheap and very reliable. Examples: IBM PC, Apple Macintosh, and every modern PC and laptop |
   | Fifth | Present and future | ULSI and Artificial Intelligence | Parallel processing, natural language input, learning and reasoning, expert systems, speech and image recognition, robotics; research into quantum and optical computing. Languages: Prolog, LISP |

   - The overall trend across the generations: size, cost and power consumption fall steeply, while speed, storage capacity and reliability rise steeply.
12. **Write short note : SMTP, RIP, RDBMS, ITSQN** *[Sheikh Kamal IT Training & Incubation Center Assistant Programmer/Instructor 04.08.2023 compact it 599 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer:

   SMTP, Simple Mail Transfer Protocol:
   - The application layer protocol used to send and relay email, running over TCP port 25, and port 587 for client submission. It is a push protocol that carries the message from the sender's client to the sender's mail server and then from one mail server to the next, using the commands HELO, MAIL FROM, RCPT TO, DATA and QUIT. It is not used to receive mail; POP3 or IMAP does that.

   RIP, Routing Information Protocol:
   - A distance vector interior gateway routing protocol that uses the Bellman-Ford algorithm. Its metric is hop count, with a maximum of 15, so 16 means unreachable. It broadcasts its whole routing table to its neighbours every 30 seconds. It uses split horizon, route poisoning and hold-down timers to limit routing loops. It is simple but slow to converge, so it is used only in small networks. Administrative distance 120.

   RDBMS, Relational Database Management System:
   - Software that stores data in tables of rows and columns and manages the relationships between them, based on Codd's relational model. It enforces primary and foreign keys, supports SQL, and guarantees the ACID properties of atomicity, consistency, isolation and durability. Examples: Oracle, MySQL, PostgreSQL, Microsoft SQL Server.

   ITSQN:
   - The abbreviation as written does not correspond to a standard computing term, and it appears to be a transcription error in the question paper. The nearest standard terms are ITSM, IT Service Management, that is the practice of designing and delivering IT services, of which ITIL is the best known framework; and ISQN, which is not a recognised computing abbreviation. <!-- verify -->

17. **What is OCR? Write down the difference between OCR and OMR.** *[NPCBL Junior Assistant Manager (ICT) 2022 compact it 642 (ET: BUET)]*


   Answer:

   What OCR is:
   - OCR stands for Optical Character Recognition. It is the technology that converts an image of text, such as a scanned document, a photograph or a PDF, into machine readable and editable text.
   - It works by scanning the image, segmenting it into lines, words and individual characters, comparing each character shape against known patterns or classifying it with a trained neural network, and finally applying a dictionary and language model to correct the result.
   - Uses: digitising printed books and records, reading passports and national identity cards, automatic number plate recognition, cheque processing in banks, and converting scanned files into searchable PDFs.

   Difference between OCR and OMR:

   | Point | OCR | OMR |
   |---|---|---|
   | Full form | Optical Character Recognition | Optical Mark Recognition |
   | What it reads | The shapes of printed or handwritten characters | The presence or absence of a mark in a predefined position |
   | Output | Actual text that can be edited and searched | Which box was filled, that is a selected option |
   | Complexity | High, it must recognise thousands of character shapes | Low, it only detects light and dark in fixed positions |
   | Accuracy | Very good for clean printed text, weaker for handwriting | Extremely high, effectively error free |
   | Speed | Slower | Very fast |
   | Special paper | Not required, any document will do | A pre-printed form with marked positions is required |
   | Examples | Digitising a book, reading a passport, number plate recognition | MCQ answer sheets, survey forms, lottery tickets, ballot papers |

   - A third related technology is MICR, Magnetic Ink Character Recognition, used on bank cheques, which reads characters printed in magnetic ink and is almost impossible to forge.

19. **Write short notes on: (i) RAM (ii) ROM (iii) Primary key (iv) Foreign key (v) Data** *[BPSC (Ministry of Home Affairs) Assistant Database Administrator (ICT) 2022 compact it 671 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer:

   (i) RAM, Random Access Memory:
   - The main working memory of a computer, in which the operating system, the running programs and the data currently in use are held.
   - It is volatile, so everything is lost when the power is switched off, and it is read and write.
   - Any location can be reached in the same time, which is what random access means.
   - Two types: SRAM, which uses flip flops, is very fast and expensive and is used for cache; and DRAM, which uses capacitors, needs periodic refreshing, and is cheaper and denser, so it is used for main memory.

   (ii) ROM, Read Only Memory:
   - Non-volatile memory whose contents survive a power failure, used to hold firmware such as the BIOS and the bootstrap program.
   - It is normally read only in operation, so the essential start-up code cannot be altered accidentally.
   - Types: PROM, written once by the user; EPROM, erasable by ultraviolet light; and EEPROM and flash memory, erasable electrically, which is what modern BIOS chips and SSDs use.

   (iii) Primary key:
   - A column, or a combination of columns, that uniquely identifies each row of a table.
   - It cannot be NULL and cannot contain duplicates, and a table may have only one primary key.
   - It automatically creates a unique index, so lookups by the key are fast.
   - Example: `student_id` in a `Student` table. A key made of more than one column is called a composite key, and a candidate key not chosen as the primary key is an alternate key.

   (iv) Foreign key:
   - A column in one table whose values must match the primary key of another table, which is how a relationship between the two tables is expressed.
   - It enforces referential integrity: a row cannot refer to a parent row that does not exist, and a parent row cannot be deleted while children refer to it, unless ON DELETE CASCADE or SET NULL is specified.
   - It may contain NULL and may contain duplicate values, and a table may have many foreign keys.
   - Example: `department_id` in an `Employee` table referring to `department_id` in a `Department` table.

   (v) Data:
   - Raw, unorganised facts and figures that by themselves carry no meaning, such as numbers, characters, symbols, images or signals.
   - When data is processed, organised and given context it becomes information, and information understood and applied becomes knowledge.
   - Example: `85`, `90`, `78` are data; "the average mark of the class is 84.3" is information.

21. **What is first programming language?** *[BARC Data Entry Officer 10.09.2022 compact it 703 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer: FORTRAN, developed by John Backus at IBM in 1957, is generally regarded as the first high level programming language.

   - Its name stands for FORmula TRANslation, and it was designed for scientific and engineering calculation. It is still used today for high performance numerical computing.
   - Two qualifications worth stating:
   - Plankalkül, designed by Konrad Zuse between 1942 and 1945, was theoretically the first high level language, but it was never implemented at the time, so it had no practical influence.
   - Before FORTRAN, programming was done in machine language, which is the binary code the processor executes directly, and then in assembly language, which uses mnemonics such as ADD and MOV.
   - Other early languages: LISP in 1958 for artificial intelligence, and COBOL in 1959 for business data processing.
   - Ada Lovelace is credited with writing the first algorithm intended for a machine, in 1843, which is why she is called the first programmer, but that was not a programming language.

23. **Write short answer on the following: (a) Plaintext (b) HTTP (c) Gateway used in \underline{\phantom{\text{Network}}} layer. (d) VIRUS full form (e) Who is the father of Computer? (f) What is Cyber security? (g) What is Malware? (h) Write the IEEE standards WiFi and WiMax. (i) Write two HTML empty tag.** *[CAAB Assistant Maintenance Engineer (AME) 2022 compact it 724 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer:

   (a) Plaintext:
   - The original readable message before encryption, or the message after it has been decrypted. Its encrypted form is called ciphertext. In web terms, data sent over HTTP travels as plaintext and can be read by anyone in the path, which is why HTTPS is used.

   (b) HTTP:
   - HyperText Transfer Protocol, the application layer protocol that carries web pages and resources over TCP port 80. It is a stateless request and response protocol; cookies or sessions are needed to maintain state. Its main methods are GET, POST, PUT and DELETE.

   (c) A gateway is used in which layer:
   - A gateway can operate at any layer of the OSI model, and it is most often described as working at the Application layer, layer 7, because it performs protocol conversion, which may require the whole message to be rebuilt in another format. In everyday LAN usage the phrase default gateway refers to a router interface, which works at the Network layer, layer 3.

   (d) VIRUS full form:
   - Vital Information Resources Under Seize.

   (e) Who is the father of the computer:
   - Charles Babbage, for designing the Difference Engine and the Analytical Engine in the nineteenth century. Alan Turing is called the father of computer science and artificial intelligence, and John von Neumann is credited with the stored program architecture used by every modern computer.

   (f) What is cyber security:
   - The practice of protecting computers, networks, programs and data from unauthorised access, damage or disruption. Its three core objectives are confidentiality, integrity and availability, known as the CIA triad. It covers network security, application security, endpoint security, identity and access management, encryption, incident response and user awareness.

   (g) What is malware:
   - Malicious software written to damage, disrupt or gain unauthorised access to a computer system. Its types include viruses, which attach to a host program; worms, which spread by themselves; Trojans, which disguise themselves as legitimate software; ransomware, which encrypts data and demands payment; spyware and keyloggers, which steal information; adware; rootkits, which hide deep in the system; and botnets.

   (h) IEEE standards for Wi-Fi and WiMAX:
   - Wi-Fi is IEEE 802.11, with amendments a, b, g, n which is Wi-Fi 4, ac which is Wi-Fi 5, ax which is Wi-Fi 6 and be which is Wi-Fi 7.
   - WiMAX is IEEE 802.16, with 802.16e being the mobile version.

   (i) Two empty HTML tags:
   - `<br>`, which inserts a line break, and `<img>`, which inserts an image. Other empty, that is self closing, tags are `<hr>`, `<input>`, `<meta>` and `<link>`. An empty tag has no closing tag and no content.

26. **“Copy and Paste” এর উদ্ভাবক কে?** *[BWMRI Assistant Maintenance Engineer 2022 compact it 736 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer: Larry Tesler is credited with inventing cut, copy and paste.

## Hardware Components & BIOS (CMOS Battery) (21)

1. **Performance of CMOS battery?** *[National Legal Aid Services Organization Assistant Maintenance Engineer 18.10.2025 compact it 1450 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer: The CMOS battery is a small 3 volt lithium coin cell, normally a CR2032, mounted on the motherboard.

   Its function:
   - It supplies power to the CMOS memory chip and to the real time clock when the computer is switched off and unplugged.
   - The CMOS memory holds the BIOS settings: the system date and time, the boot device order, hardware configuration, the CPU and RAM settings, and any BIOS password.
   - Without it, all these settings would be lost the moment the mains power was removed, and the machine would have to be reconfigured at every start-up.

   Performance and life:
   - A healthy CMOS battery lasts about 3 to 5 years in normal use, and longer in a machine that is left plugged in, because the CMOS then draws its power from the standby rail rather than the battery.
   - Its voltage should measure about 3.0 volts; below about 2.5 volts it should be replaced.
   - It is not rechargeable, so it is simply replaced rather than recharged.

   Symptoms of a failing CMOS battery:
   - The system date and time reset to a default value at every start-up.
   - BIOS settings, especially the boot order, are lost, so the machine may fail to boot or boot from the wrong device.
   - An error message such as "CMOS checksum error", "CMOS battery failure" or "System time not set" appears at start-up.
   - Certificate and licensing errors and HTTPS warnings appear, because the wrong system date makes valid certificates look expired.
   - The machine may take longer to start, as the BIOS re-detects hardware each time.

   - Replacement is simple: shut down, unplug the power, remove the old cell, fit a new CR2032 with the positive side upward, and then re-enter the BIOS settings such as the date, time and boot order.
2. **(c) Explain the rule of BIOS (Basic Input Output System) in the boot process of a PC. Describe the steps involved in booting a computer from power on to loading operating system.** *[BPSC (Ministry of Power, Energy & Mineral Resources) Assistant Director (ICT) (CS/CSE) 29.05.2025 compact it 1356 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer:

   Role of the BIOS in the boot process:

   - BIOS stands for Basic Input Output System. It is firmware stored in a ROM or flash chip on the motherboard, and it is the first software that runs when a computer is switched on.
   - Its functions are: to run the Power On Self Test, which checks the CPU, RAM, keyboard, display and storage; to initialise and configure the hardware; to provide low level input and output routines that the operating system can use; to read the boot order from CMOS and load the bootloader from the selected device; and to hand control to the operating system.
   - Its settings, such as boot order, date and time, and hardware options, are held in CMOS memory and kept alive by a small battery.
   - UEFI, Unified Extensible Firmware Interface, has replaced BIOS in modern machines, offering GPT support for disks larger than 2 TB, a graphical setup, faster booting and Secure Boot.

   Steps in booting a computer, from power on to loading the operating system:
   - Step 1, power on and Power Good signal: the power supply stabilises and sends a Power Good signal to the motherboard, which releases the CPU from reset.
   - Step 2, CPU starts execution: the CPU begins executing instructions at a fixed address in the BIOS ROM, called the reset vector.
   - Step 3, POST, the Power On Self Test: the BIOS checks the CPU, the RAM, the keyboard, the display adapter and the storage controllers. A failure is reported with beep codes or POST codes, because the display may not yet be usable.
   - Step 4, hardware initialisation: the BIOS initialises the chipset, the memory controller, the graphics adapter and the peripheral controllers, and builds a table of the devices found.
   - Step 5, reading CMOS settings: the BIOS reads the saved configuration from CMOS, including the date and time and the boot device order.
   - Step 6, selecting the boot device: the BIOS tries each device in the configured order, that is the SSD, the hard disk, the USB drive, the optical drive or the network, until it finds one with a valid boot signature.
   - Step 7, loading the bootloader: the BIOS reads the first sector of the chosen device, that is the 512 byte Master Boot Record, into memory at address 0x7C00 and checks for the signature 0x55AA. On a UEFI system it instead reads the EFI boot loader file from the EFI System Partition.
   - Step 8, the bootloader runs: GRUB on Linux or the Windows Boot Manager takes over, presents a menu if several operating systems are installed, and loads the operating system kernel into memory.
   - Step 9, the kernel initialises: it takes control of the hardware, sets up memory management and interrupts, loads the device drivers, and mounts the root file system.
   - Step 10, system initialisation: the kernel starts the first user process, `init` or `systemd` on Linux and the Session Manager on Windows, which starts the system services.
   - Step 11, the login prompt or desktop appears, and the machine is ready for use.

   - The whole sequence is called bootstrapping, from the idea of a machine pulling itself up by its own bootstraps, since a small piece of firmware loads a larger loader which loads the whole operating system.

   Answer: Three components that most affect the performance of a computer system:

   1. Processor, the CPU:
   - Clock speed in GHz determines how many cycles are executed per second, and the number of cores and threads determines how many tasks can run truly in parallel.
   - Cache size matters greatly, because a hit in L1, L2 or L3 cache is many times faster than fetching from main memory.
   - Architecture and instruction set efficiency, that is instructions per clock, often matter more than raw clock speed; a newer 3 GHz processor easily outperforms an older 4 GHz one.
   - A processor that is too slow becomes the bottleneck for computation, compilation and video encoding.

   2. Primary memory, the RAM:
   - Capacity is decisive. If there is not enough RAM the operating system swaps pages to disk, and because disk access is orders of magnitude slower than memory, the whole system becomes sluggish. This is called thrashing and it is the single commonest cause of a slow computer.
   - Speed and type also matter, that is DDR4 against DDR5, the clock frequency, and the number of channels, since dual channel gives roughly double the bandwidth of single channel.

   3. Secondary storage:
   - The type of drive is the largest single difference a user notices. An SSD gives access times of about 0.1 ms and hundreds of megabytes to several gigabytes per second, while a mechanical hard disk gives about 10 ms and 100 to 200 MB/s. Replacing a hard disk with an SSD transforms boot time and application launch time.
   - The interface matters too: NVMe over PCI Express is far faster than SATA.
   - Free space also affects performance, since a nearly full drive fragments and leaves no room for the swap file.

   - Other factors worth naming briefly: the bus width and speed, the graphics card for graphical work, the number of programs running at once, malware, the age of the operating system installation, and cooling, because an overheating processor throttles its own clock speed to protect itself.

20. **(d) Mention and discuss some fectors that affect the processing speed a computer.** *[BPSC Assistant Maintenance Engineer (ICT) 2020 compact it 1024-1025 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer: Factors that affect the processing speed of a computer:

   - CPU clock speed: the number of cycles per second, measured in GHz. More cycles means more instructions executed per second, all else being equal.
   - Number of cores and threads: multiple cores execute several tasks genuinely in parallel, which matters greatly for modern multitasking and multithreaded software.

   - RAM speed and channels: the memory clock, the generation, DDR4 or DDR5, and single against dual channel operation.
   - Secondary storage type: an SSD gives access in about 0.1 ms against about 10 ms for a mechanical disk, so boot time and application launch time change dramatically. NVMe over PCI Express is faster still than SATA.

   - Malware and fragmentation, which consume resources invisibly.
   - Cooling and thermal throttling: an overheating processor deliberately reduces its own clock speed to protect itself, so a dusty fan can halve performance.
   - Power settings: a laptop on a battery saving profile runs its processor far below its maximum speed.
21. **How to solve laptop overheating problem?** *[Microcredit Regulatory Authority Assistant Maintenance Engineer 2020 compact it 1034 (ET: BUET)]*


   Answer: Laptop overheating is solved by improving heat removal, reducing heat production and correcting the software causes.

   Cleaning and physical maintenance:
   - Clean the air vents and the internal fan with compressed air; accumulated dust blocking the exhaust is by far the commonest cause.
   - Replace the thermal paste between the processor and the heat sink. It dries out after two or three years, and replacing it can lower temperatures by 10 to 20 degrees Celsius.
   - Check that the fan is actually spinning; a seized or noisy fan must be replaced.
   - Ensure the heat sink is properly seated and its fins are not clogged.

   Usage and environment:
   - Place the laptop on a hard flat surface, never on a bed, a sofa or a lap, since soft surfaces block the intake vents underneath.
   - Use a cooling pad with fans, or a simple stand that raises the rear of the machine to improve airflow.
   - Keep the room reasonably cool and avoid direct sunlight.

   Software and configuration:
   - Close unnecessary background programs and browser tabs, and check the task manager for a process consuming the CPU continuously.
   - Scan for malware, since cryptomining and botnet malware keep the processor at full load.
   - Update the BIOS and the chipset, graphics and power management drivers, which often contain fan curve and thermal fixes.
   - Set the power plan to balanced rather than high performance, and limit the maximum processor state to about 90 percent, which cuts heat sharply for very little loss of speed.
   - Undervolt the CPU with the manufacturer's tool where it is supported, which reduces heat without reducing performance.
   - Disable unnecessary start-up programs.

   Hardware:
   - Replace a failing battery, since a swollen or faulty battery generates heat and can be dangerous.
   - Consider replacing a mechanical hard disk with an SSD, which runs cooler and consumes less power.
   - If the machine still overheats after all this, the heat pipe may be damaged and the cooling assembly should be replaced by a service centre.

   - Monitoring: use a tool such as HWMonitor or Core Temp to watch the temperature. Under load, 70 to 85 degrees Celsius is normal for a laptop CPU; sustained operation above 90 degrees causes throttling and shortens the life of the components.

7. **Copyright আইন কি? এর প্রয়োজনীয়তা ব্যাখ্যা করুন।** *[DMLC Assistant Teacher (ICT) 2021 compact it 826 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer:

   What copyright law is:
   - Copyright is the legal right granted to the creator of an original work over the reproduction, distribution, adaptation, public performance and display of that work. It is a form of intellectual property.
   - It protects the expression of an idea, not the idea itself. A story, a piece of music, a photograph, a film, a painting, a design and computer software are all protected; the underlying idea or fact is not.
   - It arises automatically as soon as the work is created and fixed in some tangible form; registration is not required for protection, although it makes proof much easier in a dispute.
   - In Bangladesh it is governed by the Copyright Act, administered by the Copyright Office, and protection generally lasts for the life of the author plus sixty years.
   - The rights are both economic, that is the right to earn from the work, and moral, that is the right to be named as the author and to object to distortion of the work.

   Why copyright law is necessary:
   - It protects the creator's economic interest, so that the person who invested time, skill and money in producing the work receives the income from it rather than whoever copies it first.
   - It creates the incentive to create. Without protection there would be little reason to invest years in writing software, producing a film or publishing a book, since a copy costs nothing to make.
   - It protects moral rights: the author is credited, and the work is not distorted or misattributed.
   - It gives a legal remedy against piracy, plagiarism and unauthorised commercial use, with civil damages and criminal penalties.
   - It underpins whole industries: publishing, music, film, and above all the software industry, which in Bangladesh is a major export earner.
   - It encourages foreign investment and technology transfer, since companies will not bring valuable intellectual property into a country that cannot protect it.
   - It enables licensing, so that a work can be shared on terms the creator chooses, which is how open source licences and Creative Commons operate.
   - It maintains a balance with the public interest through fair dealing exceptions for research, criticism, news reporting and education, and through the eventual expiry of the term, after which the work enters the public domain.

12. **What is ERP? Write down the Implementation Challenges of ERP?** *[BREB Junior Assistant Manager (ICT) 2021 compact it 948 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer:

   What ERP is:
   - ERP stands for Enterprise Resource Planning. It is integrated software that manages all the core business processes of an organisation — finance, human resources, procurement, inventory, manufacturing, sales and customer relations — through a single shared database.
   - Because every module writes to the same database, data is entered once and is immediately visible everywhere, which removes the duplication and the reconciliation problems of separate departmental systems.
   - Its benefits: a single version of the truth, real time reporting across the whole organisation, standardised processes, better inventory and cash management, easier regulatory compliance and audit, and reduced manual work.
   - Examples: SAP, Oracle ERP Cloud, Microsoft Dynamics 365, Odoo and ERPNext.

   Implementation challenges of ERP:
   - High cost: licences, hardware, consultants, customisation, data migration and training, which frequently exceed the original budget several times over.
   - Long duration: a full implementation commonly takes one to three years, during which business requirements themselves change.
   - Resistance to change: staff are attached to familiar processes and fear that transparency will expose inefficiency or reduce their discretion. This is the single largest cause of failure.
   - Business process re-engineering: ERP imposes standard processes, so the organisation must usually change how it works rather than the software. Deciding what to change and what to customise is difficult and political.
   - Over-customisation: modifying the package heavily makes upgrades expensive or impossible and destroys the benefit of using standard software.
   - Data migration and data quality: years of inconsistent, duplicated and incomplete data in legacy systems must be cleansed and mapped before it can be loaded.
   - Integration with existing systems that will not be replaced, such as a core banking system or a specialised production system.
   - Inadequate training: users who do not understand the system enter wrong data, and the reports then become useless.
   - Lack of top management commitment: without visible sponsorship the project loses priority the moment it becomes difficult.
   - Unclear requirements and scope creep, which extend the timeline and the cost indefinitely.
   - Choosing the wrong vendor or the wrong product for the size and sector of the organisation.
   - Dependence on consultants, so that knowledge leaves when they leave.
   - Performance and infrastructure problems, particularly where network connectivity between branches is weak.
   - Post go-live support: the period immediately after cut-over is the most difficult, and productivity typically dips before it improves.

   - Critical success factors: strong executive sponsorship, a dedicated cross functional project team, phased rather than big bang roll-out where possible, minimal customisation, thorough data cleansing beforehand, and continuous training and change management.

16. **RFID has huge applications in business, especially in supply chain management and toll collection system. Show the basic working principle of RFID in brief.** *[Sonali & Janata Bank Officer (IT) 2020 compact it 987-988 (ET: DU)]* *[Bangladesh Bank Recruitment Test 2020 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer: RFID stands for Radio Frequency Identification. It identifies and tracks objects automatically using radio waves, without needing line of sight or physical contact.

   Components:
   - Tag, also called a transponder: a microchip holding a unique identifier and some data, attached to an antenna and fixed to the object. It may be passive, with no battery, drawing its power from the reader's radio field; active, with its own battery and a range of up to 100 metres; or semi-passive.
   - Reader, also called an interrogator: transmits a radio signal, powers the passive tag, receives its reply and passes the data on.
   - Antenna: on both the tag and the reader, which couples the energy and the data.
   - Middleware and host system: filters the raw reads, removes duplicates and passes clean events to the application database.

   Working principle:
   - Step 1: the reader continuously transmits a radio frequency signal through its antenna, creating an electromagnetic field.
   - Step 2: when a tag enters that field, the tag's antenna picks up the energy. In a passive tag this induced current is what powers the chip; no battery is needed.
   - Step 3: the powered chip modulates and reflects the signal back, a technique called backscatter, encoding its unique identifier and any stored data.
   - Step 4: the reader receives and decodes the reflected signal and sends the identifier to the host system.
   - Step 5: the host looks the identifier up in a database and takes the appropriate action, for example recording that a carton has left the warehouse or deducting a toll from an account.
   - Anti-collision protocols allow the reader to read hundreds of tags in the field at once by making them respond in staggered time slots, which is the key advantage over barcodes.

   ```mermaid
   graph LR
       A["RFID Reader + Antenna"] -->|"RF signal, powers the tag"| B["RFID Tag on the object"]
       B -->|"Backscattered ID and data"| A
       A --> C["Middleware: filtering and de-duplication"]
       C --> D["Host application and database"]
   ```

   Frequency bands: low frequency at 125 kHz for animal tagging and access control; high frequency at 13.56 MHz for NFC, smart cards and library books; ultra high frequency at 860 to 960 MHz for supply chain and toll collection, with a range of several metres.

   Application in supply chain management:
   - Cartons and pallets are tagged, and readers at the loading bay read an entire pallet in one pass without unpacking it, which removes manual scanning entirely.
   - Real time visibility of inventory at every stage, from manufacture to warehouse to shop shelf, so stock levels, shrinkage and expiry are known continuously.
   - Automatic goods receipt and dispatch, faster stock taking, and prevention of counterfeit goods entering the chain.

   Application in toll collection:
   - A tag is fixed to the vehicle windscreen and linked to a prepaid account.
   - As the vehicle approaches, the overhead reader identifies the tag at speed, the system deducts the toll automatically and raises the barrier, or in an open road system charges without any barrier at all.
   - A camera captures the number plate as a fallback for vehicles without a valid tag.
   - The result is no stopping, no cash handling, shorter queues, less fuel wasted and a complete audit trail. This is how ETC systems such as E-ZPass and FASTag operate.

   Advantages over barcodes: no line of sight required, many tags read simultaneously, greater range, data can be rewritten, tags survive dirt and rough handling, and each item has a unique identity rather than a product type.
   Limitations: higher cost per tag, interference from metal and liquid, privacy concerns, and the lack of a single global frequency standard.
17. **a) What is E-Government? How can E-Government be implemented through the vision of Digital Bangladesh?** *[BPSC Assistant Maintenance Engineer (ICT) 2020 compact it 1031 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer:

   What e-government is:
   - E-government is the use of information and communication technology by government to deliver services, share information and conduct transactions with citizens, businesses and other government agencies, more efficiently, transparently and accessibly than by traditional means.
   - Its categories are G2C, government to citizen; G2B, government to business; G2G, government to government; and G2E, government to employee.
   - Its objectives: faster and cheaper service delivery, transparency and reduced corruption, accountability, citizen participation, and reaching people who cannot travel to an office.

   How e-government can be implemented through the vision of Digital Bangladesh:
   - Digital Bangladesh, declared in 2009 as part of Vision 2021, rests on four pillars: human resource development, connecting citizens, digital government, and ICT in business. E-government is the third pillar, and it depends on the other three.
   - Connectivity infrastructure: the Info-Sarker programme has connected union parishads with optical fibre, and the submarine cables and the national backbone provide the international bandwidth. Without connectivity at the union level, digital service delivery cannot reach the village.
   - Union Digital Centres, now Union Digital Service Centres: more than four thousand one-stop centres run by local entrepreneurs, where a villager who owns no computer can obtain a birth certificate, a land record, an application form or a passport appointment. This is the single most important delivery mechanism for e-government in Bangladesh.
   - National portal and integrated service delivery: `bangladesh.gov.bd` bringing all ministries, departments and district offices onto one platform, and MyGov as a single window for service applications.
   - Digital identity: the National ID system with biometric verification, which is the foundation that allows every other service to authenticate a citizen.
   - e-Nothi, the electronic file management system, which moves government file processing from paper to digital, so that a file can be tracked, cannot be lost, and its delay can be attributed.
   - e-GP, electronic government procurement, which conducts tendering online, has substantially reduced collusion and irregularity, and is one of the country's clearest e-government successes.
   - Digital payment: online payment of taxes, VAT, utility bills and government fees through mobile financial services, and government to person payments such as stipends and allowances paid directly into a beneficiary's mobile wallet.
   - Sector applications: online passport and visa, land record digitisation, online birth and death registration, digital land mutation, online public examination results, and the 333 and 999 helplines.
   - Capacity building: training government officers in ICT, appointing ICT focal points in every office, and establishing the a2i programme to drive innovation across ministries.
   - Legal framework: the ICT Act, the Digital Security Act and the data protection framework, together with rules giving digital documents and signatures legal validity.
   - Interoperability and shared data: a national enterprise architecture and a data sharing framework, so that a citizen does not have to supply the same document to five different offices.
   - Monitoring and citizen feedback: service process simplification, service delivery innovation funds, and grievance redress systems such as GRS.

   - Requirements for success: political commitment, sustained funding, cyber security, digital literacy, addressing the digital divide so that rural and poor citizens are not excluded, and above all process simplification, because digitising a bad process only produces a faster bad process.
18. **b) List some factors that are needed to implement Digital Bangladesh.** *[BPSC Assistant Maintenance Engineer (ICT) 2020 compact it 1031 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer: Factors needed to implement Digital Bangladesh:

   - Connectivity infrastructure: nationwide optical fibre reaching every union, affordable broadband, adequate international bandwidth through the submarine cables, and mobile coverage with 4G and 5G.
   - Reliable electricity: uninterrupted power supply, since none of the digital infrastructure functions without it, particularly in rural areas.
   - Human resource development: ICT education from school level, trained teachers, universities producing qualified graduates, and continuous training for government officials and for the workforce.
   - Digital literacy of citizens: the ability to use a smartphone, a portal and a digital payment, without which services exist but are not used.
   - Affordable devices and data: financing schemes for computers and smartphones, and data packages that ordinary households can afford.
   - Digital government systems: e-Nothi, e-GP, the national portal, integrated databases and the Union Digital Centres that deliver services locally.
   - National digital identity: the NID with biometric verification, which authenticates citizens for every other service.
   - Digital payment ecosystem: mobile financial services, internet banking, the national payment switch and interoperability between them.
   - Legal and regulatory framework: the ICT Act, the Digital Security Act, data protection law, recognition of digital signatures and electronic records, and consumer protection for e-commerce.
   - Cyber security: a national CERT, security operations centres, secure government data centres and trained security professionals, because trust collapses after a single serious breach.
   - Data centre and cloud infrastructure: the national data centre and the tier IV data centre at Kaliakoir, with disaster recovery.
   - Local software and ICT industry: software parks, incentives for the ICT sector, and encouragement of freelancing, which is already a major earner.
   - Political commitment and sustained funding, with a clear institutional home such as the a2i programme and the ICT Division.
   - Process re-engineering: simplifying the underlying service before digitising it, since automating a bad process merely makes it fail faster.
   - Addressing the digital divide: rural areas, women, the poor and people with disabilities must be brought in deliberately, or the programme increases inequality.
   - Public private partnership, and coordination between ministries so that systems interoperate instead of duplicating one another.
   - Monitoring, evaluation and citizen feedback, so that services are improved on evidence rather than assumption.

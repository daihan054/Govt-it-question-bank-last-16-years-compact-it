1. Focus Writing in English: "The Role of Sustainable Banking in Achieving the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) in Bangladesh." *[Officer (IT) 31 Jul 2026 bscs 01 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer: The Role of Sustainable Banking in Achieving the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) in Bangladesh

   Sustainable banking means directing credit and investment towards activities that protect the environment, promote social equity and remain financially sound. For Bangladesh, a country ranked among the most climate-vulnerable in the world, it is not an option but a necessity.

   Bangladesh Bank has been a pioneer in this field. Its Green Banking Policy, the Sustainable Finance Department, the Green Transformation Fund and the mandatory sustainability rating of banks have pushed the whole sector towards responsible lending. Banks now finance solar home systems, effluent treatment plants, green factories and energy-efficient brick kilns.

   Challenges remain: greenwashing, weak monitoring, limited technical capacity to appraise green projects and the higher cost of long-term finance. Stronger disclosure rules, trained assessors and concessional refinancing can overcome them.

   Sustainable banking turns the financial sector into an instrument of national development rather than a mere lender.

3. **(e) Write a short paragraph arguing for or against the statement "AI will eventually replace human workers in most professions."** *[NPCBL Sub Assistant Engineer: Cyber Security Analyst Date: 11 July 2026 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer: Against the statement: AI will not replace human workers in most professions.

   Artificial intelligence is transforming work, but the claim that it will replace humans in most professions overstates what the technology actually does. AI is a pattern-matching tool. It is superb at repetitive, data-heavy and rule-bound tasks, and weak wherever judgement, empathy, physical dexterity or accountability is required.

   History supports this. The automated teller machine was expected to end the career of the bank teller, yet the number of tellers rose for two decades because branches became cheaper to run and multiplied. Spreadsheets did not end accountancy; they moved accountants from arithmetic to analysis. Technology has consistently shifted the content of jobs rather than removing them.

   Most professions are bundles of tasks, not single tasks. A doctor reads scans, but also examines patients, weighs uncertain evidence, explains bad news and bears legal responsibility. AI can take over the first task and leave the rest untouched. The same applies to teachers, lawyers, engineers and nurses.

   The real risk is not mass unemployment but polarisation. Routine clerical and data-entry roles will shrink sharply, and those workers need retraining, not reassurance.

   The rational conclusion is that AI will replace tasks, augment workers and create new occupations, provided governments invest seriously in reskilling.
4. **Paragraph on: Three Zero theory** *[BREB Assistant Programmer (AP) 21.02.2025 compact it 1336 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer: The Three Zero Theory

   The Three Zero theory was proposed by Nobel laureate Professor Muhammad Yunus. It sets out a vision of a civilisation built on three targets: zero poverty, zero unemployment and zero net carbon emission.

   Zero poverty means that poverty is not created by poor people but by the economic system around them. If credit, skills and markets reach everyone, no one need remain poor. Microcredit, which reached millions of rural women in Bangladesh, was the first practical test of this idea.

   Zero unemployment rests on the belief that every human being is born an entrepreneur, not a job seeker. Instead of waiting for employers, young people should be supported with seed capital, mentoring and social business funds so that they create jobs for themselves and others.

   Zero net carbon emission demands that consumption and production stop damaging the planet. Renewable energy, recycling and responsible consumption are essential, because the poorest countries, including Bangladesh, suffer most from a crisis they did not cause.

   The instrument for achieving all three is the social business: a company that is financially self-sustaining but returns no dividend, reinvesting all profit in its social mission.

   For a young and densely populated country like Bangladesh, the Three Zero theory offers a practical roadmap in which growth, employment and environmental protection advance together rather than against one another.

   Answer: Corruption in the ICT Sector in Bangladesh

   Introduction: The ICT sector is the engine of the Digital Bangladesh programme, but it has not escaped the corruption that weakens other public services. Because ICT projects involve large budgets and highly technical specifications, they are unusually easy to manipulate.

   Types of corruption: Tender manipulation and collusive bidding; specifications written around one brand so that only one supplier qualifies; over-invoicing of hardware and software licences; ghost projects that exist only on paper; bribery for licences, domain approvals and connectivity permissions; recruitment irregularities in technical posts; and misuse of public data for private gain.

7. **Write short note on:** "The role of AI and machine language mitigate challenges of cyber attack on banking system" 100 to 150 Words. *[Bangladesh Bank Assistant Director (ICT) 07.02.2025 compact it 1324 (ET: DU)]*


   Answer: The Role of AI and Machine Learning in Mitigating Cyber Attacks on the Banking System

   Banks are among the most attacked institutions in the world, and the volume of transactions has grown far beyond what human analysts can monitor. Artificial intelligence and machine learning have therefore become essential to banking security.

   Their first strength is anomaly detection. A model learns the normal behaviour of each customer, such as usual amounts, locations, devices and times, and flags any transaction that deviates from that baseline. This catches card fraud and account takeover within milliseconds, before the money leaves the system.

   Their second strength is speed and scale. Machine learning engines examine millions of log entries, network flows and ATM events in real time, identifying phishing sites, malware traffic and insider misuse that signature-based tools would miss.

   They also support behavioural biometrics, adaptive authentication, automated incident response and the prediction of new attack patterns from global threat intelligence.

   The limits must be acknowledged. Models produce false positives, they can be poisoned with bad training data, attackers now use AI themselves, and explainability is required by regulators.

   Used with skilled human oversight, AI and machine learning shift bank security from reacting after a loss to preventing it.
8. **Roadside tree plantation in reducing noise pollution.** *[Sonali Bank PLC Assistant Database Administrator 23.02.2024 compact it 312 (ET: N/A)]*


   Answer: Roadside Tree Plantation in Reducing Noise Pollution

   Noise pollution has become a serious health hazard in Bangladeshi cities. Dhaka regularly records 90 to 100 decibels on its main roads, far above the 60 decibel limit set for a commercial area. Constant exposure causes hearing loss, high blood pressure, sleeplessness, irritability and poor concentration in students.

   Roadside tree plantation is one of the simplest and cheapest defences. A well-designed belt of trees works in three ways. The leaves and branches absorb sound energy, especially at the higher frequencies that the ear finds most irritating. The rough surfaces of trunks and foliage scatter sound waves so that they lose energy in many directions. The soft ground and undergrowth beneath the trees absorb the ground-borne component instead of reflecting it as concrete does.

   Research shows that a dense green belt of about ten metres depth can reduce noise by five to ten decibels, which the ear perceives as roughly halving the loudness. Evergreen species with broad, thick leaves and dense branching close to the ground, planted in staggered rows, give the best result.

   The additional benefits are considerable: shade, dust filtration, carbon absorption, lower urban temperature and a more pleasant streetscape.

   City corporations should therefore treat roadside plantation as essential infrastructure rather than decoration.

11. **Focus in English: “Impacts of air pollution on human health” within 150 (+-10%) word.** *[Combined Bank Assistant Maintenance Engineer/ Assistant Engineer (IT) 24.02.2024 compact it 312 (ET: BIBM)]*


    Answer: Impacts of Air Pollution on Human Health

    Air pollution is now the leading environmental cause of death in Bangladesh. Dhaka is repeatedly ranked among the most polluted cities in the world, with fine particulate levels many times the World Health Organization guideline.

    The main pollutants are PM2.5 and PM10 from brick kilns, vehicle exhaust and construction dust, together with sulphur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide, ozone and lead.

    The health effects are severe. Fine particles penetrate deep into the lungs and enter the bloodstream, causing asthma, chronic bronchitis, emphysema and lung cancer. They inflame blood vessels and raise the risk of heart attack and stroke. Carbon monoxide reduces the oxygen carried by the blood, producing headache, fatigue and impaired judgement. Lead damages the developing brain and permanently lowers the intelligence of children. Pregnant women exposed to polluted air are more likely to deliver underweight or premature babies. Recent research also links long-term exposure to dementia and depression.

    Children, the elderly, traffic police, rickshaw pullers and street vendors suffer most because they breathe the worst air for the longest time.

    Cleaner brick technology, stricter vehicle emission testing, covered construction sites and expanded urban greenery are urgent, because every year of delay costs thousands of lives.

    Answer: The Importance of Digital Literacy in Expanding Cashless Transactions in Bangladesh

    Bangladesh has built an impressive digital payment infrastructure. Mobile financial services carry crores of taka daily, agent banking has reached the union level, and Bangladesh Bank has introduced RTGS, BEFTN, the National Payment Switch and the interoperable Binimoy platform. Yet cash still dominates. The missing element is not technology but digital literacy.

    Answer: Digital Financial Literacy

    Digital financial literacy is the ability to use digital financial services safely, confidently and to one's own advantage. It combines two skills: understanding money, and understanding the technology that now carries it.

    Digital financial literacy is what turns an account number into genuine financial inclusion.

    Answer: Edge Computing

   Edge computing is a model in which data is processed close to where it is generated instead of being sent to a distant central cloud. The processing happens on the device itself, on a nearby gateway or at a small local data centre, and only summarised or important data travels onward.

28. **Write a composition: “Block chain technology”** *[RAKUB Maintenance Engineer (PO) 05.10.2021 compact it 858 (ET: N/A)]*

    Answer: Blockchain Technology

   A blockchain is a distributed digital ledger in which records are grouped into blocks and each block is cryptographically linked to the one before it. Copies of the ledger are held by every participant in the network, so no single authority controls it.

   It works through four mechanisms. Each block carries the hash of the previous block, so altering an old record changes every hash after it and the tampering becomes obvious.    Its properties are decentralisation, immutability, transparency and security.

   The applications extend far beyond cryptocurrency. Supply chains use it to trace a product from farm to shelf. Banks use it for cross-border payment and trade finance, cutting settlement from days to minutes. Land registries use it to prevent forged titles, a serious problem in Bangladesh. Health systems use it for tamper-proof patient records, universities for verifiable certificates, and governments for auditable voting. Smart contracts execute an agreement automatically when its conditions are met.

   The limitations are real: low transaction throughput, the enormous energy consumption of proof of work, difficulty in scaling, absence of clear regulation, and the impossibility of correcting an entry made in error.

   Blockchain is best suited where several parties who do not fully trust one another must share one authoritative record.

   Answer: Transformation of an assertive sentence into other forms follows fixed rules.

   Simple to compound:
   - Rule: expand the participle, phrase or infinitive into a finite clause and join with and, but, or, so.

   - Compound: He was tired and so he went to bed early.

   - Rule: turn one of the coordinate clauses into a subordinate clause with as, since, because, though, when.

   - Rule: begin with What a / What / How, place the subject and verb after it, and end with a note of exclamation.

13. **Right forms of verb.** *[NSDA Assistant Maintenance Engineer Date: 04-03-2022 compact it 659 (ET: N/A)]*


    Answer: The right form of the verb is decided by the tense marker, the subject, the auxiliary and the structure of the sentence. The main rules are given below with examples.

    Rule 1 - a present indefinite verb takes s or es with a third person singular subject.
    - He (go) to school every day. Answer: goes.

    Rule 2 - words such as now, at present, look, listen indicate the present continuous.
    - Listen, the baby (cry). Answer: is crying.

    Rule 3 - since, for, already, just, yet, ever, never indicate the perfect tense.
    - I (know) him for ten years. Answer: have known.

    Rule 4 - a definite past time such as yesterday, ago, last night requires the past indefinite.
    - He (come) here yesterday. Answer: came.

    Rule 5 - when two past actions occur, the earlier one takes the past perfect.
    - The train (leave) before we reached the station. Answer: had left.

    Rule 6 - after had better, would better, rather than, let, make, see, hear the base form is used.
    - You had better (go) home. Answer: go.

    Rule 7 - lest is always followed by should + base form.
    - Walk fast lest you (miss) the train. Answer: should miss.

    Rule 8 - after it is time and it is high time, the past form is used.
    - It is high time we (change) our habit. Answer: changed.

    Rule 9 - after wish, as if and as though, "were" is used for all persons.
    - I wish I (be) a bird. Answer: were.

    Rule 10 - after recommend, suggest, propose, demand, insist and order, the base form is used.
    - He recommended that she (write) the letter. Answer: write.

    Rule 11 - the subject decides the number of the verb; a phrase between subject and verb does not.
    - The cost of these articles (have) risen. Answer: has.

    Rule 12 - "the + adjective" denotes a class and takes a plural verb.
    - The poor (be) born to suffer. Answer: are.

    Rule 13 - if the subject receives the action, the passive form is used.
    - The rose (call) the queen of flowers. Answer: is called.

    Rule 14 - in the first conditional, "if + present indefinite" is followed by "shall or will + base form"; in the second conditional, "if + past indefinite" is followed by "would + base form".
    - If it rains, we (stay) at home. Answer: shall stay.
    - If I were rich, I (help) the poor. Answer: would help.
